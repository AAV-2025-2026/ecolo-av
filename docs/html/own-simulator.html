Ben, 7 Nov: "merge in everything from gazebo so its standalone in rust". This page is the simulator's core: the world, the car, the lidar, the radar, the IMU, the GPS, the wheels and the clock. The cameras are their own program (its page), started beside it. The plan: the design.

pixi run sim world:=test_lot gui:=false rviz:=false     # ours: the default since 8 Nov
pixi run sim-check                                     # the road world's checks, on ours
pixi run sim simulator:=gazebo world:=test_lot         # Gazebo, as before

Ours is the default since 8 Nov: it passed every sim-check leg Gazebo passes (below). Gazebo stays, as simulator:=gazebo: to compare, and on a computer with no GPU (GitHub's CI, oirp-spss), where our cameras can't draw (sim_cameras says "no GPU to draw with") but Gazebo's can, in software (tools/software_gl.sh).

It replaces gz sim and its bridges. Every node above them is the same one Gazebo uses.

What it gives

Topic Rate Same as Gazebo?
/clock 1 kHz yes: one per step
/gazebo/odom, /vehicle/sim/truth 50 Hz yes
/gazebo/imu 50 Hz, noise 0.02 rad/s, 0.2 m/s² yes (the xacro's)
/gazebo/navsat 5 Hz, noise 4.5e-6° (0.5 m) yes; exact WGS84 from the world's datum
/joint_states 100 Hz Gazebo sends 1 kHz; the car's wheels 100 Hz
/sensors/lidar/points 10 Hz, 1800 x 16, 0.5-100 m, 3 cm yes: packed as Gazebo's, organised, inf misses
/gazebo/radar/points 10 Hz, 61 x 3, 0.3-8.4 m, 5 cm yes
the three car cameras, the chase camera 10 Hz, 640 x 360 yes: aav_sim_cameras draws the same world file

The rays see what Gazebo's lidar sees: the visuals. One roof-lidar scan takes 1.7 ms in the test lot and 15 ms on the campus (Ben's Mac, 4 threads).

The car

Part How
Today gazebo_adapter's actuator model, then Gazebo's AckermannSteering (±10 m/s², the wheels steer at once), then the bicycle
Hitting things the outline (vehicle.yaml body:) stops at what it meets, at 0.35, 0.8 and 1.3 m up, as Gazebo's physics does. The log says "the car hit something"
The ground (8 Nov) each wheel finds the ground below it (a ray straight down). The car rides up a slope and tilts on it; a step up to 0.1 m (ground_step) it rolls up; a higher one (a 0.3 m kerb) stops it, as a wall does. The truth carries the height and tilt, so the cameras follow
The trait sim-core CarModel: step, state, reset, hold. The shared car-model crate goes in behind it at the merge
ActuatorCar the actuator model itself, to car-model's rules (exact lags, rate-limited motor, deadband, dead time). Tested; not wired in yet

With simulator:=own, gazebo.launch.py starts the cameras (sim_cameras --ros --pose truth --world <the world>) and sets gazebo_adapter's cameras to true, so /system/source announces them, as with Gazebo.

The stop sign: the lock never came on while driving in 1 of the first 5 runs, and in none of the next 3. A listener logged every box: a picture every 0.1 s, no gaps, the detector's box 50 ms after each picture's stamp, growing smoothly as the car came near. No fault found in the sign's size, height or pose, or the camera's mount; the one failure's cause is not known. Our box is smaller than Gazebo's at the same place (52 px against 68); the lens's own sum gives about 49 px, so ours is the nearer.

Found and fixed on the way: with faults:=true our front left camera_info went to the relay's topic too, so --faults waited for the lens and failed; now only the picture moves (--image-topic), as Gazebo's bridge does.

So on 8 Nov DEFAULT_SIMULATOR became own. sim-check says which simulator it runs, every time (simulator:=).
