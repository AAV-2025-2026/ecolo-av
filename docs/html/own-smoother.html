Ben, 8 Nov: a velocity smoother that knows it drives a car, beside Nav2's, as the challenger. Nav2 stays the default, and stays on the car.

The short version

  • Switch: smoother:=own. Package aav_smoother (Rust).

  • What it is: Nav2's velocity_smoother for the speed (limits, acceleration, the timeout, the deadband, open or closed loop), and the car's steering for the turn.

  • Drop-in: the same node name, parameters and topics. Nothing downstream can tell.

  • Test lot, 3 runs each (all ours): the same legs (10 of 12); ego clipped 0-0.1 % of the steering against 9-15 % behind Nav2's; off the path 0.12 m against 0.15 m; the tour 701 s against 890 s. Campus not run yet.

Why

Nav2's smoother treats v and w as two separate dials, like a robot that turns on the spot. A car can't:

A car's limit Nav2's smoother Ours
The tightest turn at this speed: w ≤ v tan(full lock) / L doesn't know it never asks for more
How fast the steering actuator turns the wheels doesn't know it: w jumps the steering angle moves at most that fast
Slowing down keeps the same path scales v and w together keeps the bend w / v
A turn too tight for max_velocity[2] cuts w: the car bends less slows the car
Speeding up and braking nav2.yaml's numbers nav2.yaml's, never above the car model's

Every car number comes from vehicle.yaml through the car model; the steering rate is the tighter of the steering actuator's (actuators.steer_rate) and ego's ramp.

How it fits

follower (MPPI, mppi_lag, ...) ─▶ cmd_vel_nav ─▶ velocity_smoother ─▶ collision_monitor ─▶ /drivers/nav/cmd_vel
                                                  (Nav2's, or ours
                                                   under its name)
Name Type For
cmd_vel_nav TwistStamped in: the follower's command
/control/cmd_vel_smoothed or /drivers/nav/cmd_vel TwistStamped out: to collision_monitor, or to twist_mux

How the swap works. navigation.launch.py smoother:=own starts car_smoother with Nav2's node name, velocity_smoother, so it reads the same block of the tuned nav2.yaml and gets the same remappings. The lifecycle manager no longer waits for it: ours is not a lifecycle node.

One tick (20 Hz)

Step What
1 No command for velocity_timeout: slow to a stop, then say nothing (Nav2's)
2 The wheels' target: atan(L w / v), within full lock (held when v is about 0)
3 The speed: within min/max_velocity, slowed so the turn stays under max_velocity[2]
4 The speed moves at most max_accel faster, max_decel slower (Nav2's rule)
5 The wheels move at most steering_rate toward their target, once the car moves
6 Out: (v, v tan(steer) / L); the deadband sends small values as 0

Try it

What Command

Its tests cd ros/drivers/aav_smoother && cargo test -p car-smoother

The score's new row, "Steering commands ego had to clip": the share of ego's steering commands more than 0.03 rad from the angle it was asked for (atan(L w / v) of /control/cmd_vel). Fewer is better: the car was asked for what it can do.

  • Ego hardly ever clips ours. Nav2's smoother passes on steering the wheels can't reach that fast; ours asks only for what the steering can do.
  • Steadier: "round the building" took 44 s every time with ours; with Nav2's, two runs of three wandered (106 s, 171 s).
  • The U-turn is still the hard leg (forward only, a 14 m lane): the follower and planner's problem, not the smoother's.
  • Not run yet (cut short for car day, 9 Nov): the campus at 2.5 m/s, and tracker:=mppi_lag under Nav2's navigator. --stacks own --smoothers nav2 own --tours campus --speed 2.5 --runs 2 and --trackers mppi_lag --smoothers nav2 own.
  • Nav2 stays the default; the car always runs Nav2's.
