A costmap is a grid laid on the ground. Every cell holds a cost:

  • free (0);
  • near something (inflated, 1–252);
  • inside something (lethal, 253–254);
  • unknown (255).

Nav2's planner searches the grid for a path the car can drive. Its controller follows that path and slows down near high-cost cells. nav2_costmap_2d builds the grids from layers. The settings are in src/drivers/aav_navigation/config/nav2.yaml.

Layers now static (the map), inflation static (the map), inflation
Layers later + obstacle layer (radar/lidar), already written but not enabled + obstacle layer if needed

A 20 m window means 10 m ahead and behind, about 5 s of driving at 2 m/s, the most this car is meant to do this year. Make it larger only if the car gets faster.

  • Footprint: the car's outline, made from body in vehicle.yaml by the launch file. Nav2 checks that it doesn't enter lethal cells.
  • Inflation: 1.5 m with cost scaling 3.0. This keeps paths away from walls rather than scraping them.

Map files

A Nav2 map is an image (PGM or PNG) plus a YAML file that says how to read it. src/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/README.md covers the format line by line. In short:

  • one pixel is one cell;
  • white is free, black is blocked, grey is unknown;
  • resolution is metres per pixel;
  • origin is the map-frame position of the bottom-left pixel.

Don't paint maps by hand. Write a short spec and generate them:

tools/make_map.py src/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/example_lot.spec.yaml

A spec lists:

  • the size in metres east and north;
  • the origin;
  • blocked rectangles and free rectangles.

tools/make_map.py --help (or the top of the file) shows the format. It needs only Python and PyYAML, not ROS.

Where the map sits on the Earth

The map frame is pinned to the ground by the datum in src/localization/aav_localization/config/navsat.yaml:

  • map (0, 0) is the datum's latitude and longitude;
  • x points east and y points north.

So a point 20 m east and 5 m north of the datum is (20, 5) on the map, in Nav2 goals and in RViz. The datum in the file now is a placeholder near Carleton.

To draw the real test site:

  1. Pick the datum: a corner of the lot, or the start spot. Survey it with the GPS (average a few minutes of fixes) and put its latitude and longitude in navsat.yaml.
  2. Measure the drivable area in metres east and north of it, with a tape, or by measuring on satellite imagery from the datum.
  3. Write the spec: the size, then the blocked rectangles (buildings, curbs, parked cars).
  4. Build the map and drive phase 3 in Gazebo first (world:=test_lot pairs with example_lot).

A costmap is not a road map

The global costmap only says which cells may be driven on. Roads, junctions and routing across a city are a separate kind of map: a road graph. Two tools handle that:

  • OSRM, which the HUD already uses. It turns a destination into a route of latitude/longitude points. Those points can become a follow_gps_waypoints goal for Nav2 (see hud.md).
  • Nav2's Route Server (nav2_route). It's released for Jazzy, in Nav2 1.3.13 as of October 2026, and follows a hand-made graph of lanes or paths. Consider it if the test site becomes a set of fixed lanes.

For this year's goals (an empty costmap, then a pre-rendered test site) a grid map plus GPS waypoints is enough.
