Written 15 October 2026. The goal (Ben, 15 Oct): "fully simulate the car inside Gazebo with all the sensors, and build the full pipeline." This page says what we have, what's missing, and how the car's measurements feed in.

The short version - Most of it exists. Gazebo already plays the car: IMU, GPS, wheel speed, three cameras, the radar, the lidar. The same EKFs, Nav2, perception and HUD run on it. - The big gap is the actuators. Gazebo's car sets its speed directly, with made-up limits. It ignores the weight, the motor and the brakes. So it doesn't yet speed up, brake or steer like the real car. - The fix: an actuator model between our commands and Gazebo, filled with the numbers from the tests (tests.md, stage 4). Then the real firmware logic in the loop, as the bench already does. Both built 15 Oct: the model is on by default, the spine with spine:=true. - Measurements come first: the sheet (measure-the-car.md) for where everything is, the tests for how it moves.

Why the weight hardly matters in Gazebo today

Gazebo's Ackermann system sets the wheels' speed and limits how fast it changes (aav.gazebo.xacro: 1.5 m/s² up, 3.0 down). A heavier car gets the same numbers. For the weight to matter, the model must push with a force (from the motor) against the mass. For driving well, what matters most is the measured g: how fast the real car speeds up and stops. The tests give that directly.

3. Two ways to model the actuators

A. An actuator model in front of Gazebo (recommended first) B. Forces inside Gazebo
How a small node: throttle % → acceleration, brake % → deceleration, steering with its speed and lag; then Gazebo's speed control as today the motor's torque on the rear wheels, the brakes' torque, tyre grip, the mass
Weight matters? through the measured g (which includes it) directly
Effort about a day about a week, and tricky to tune
Good for Nav2, the smooth stop, the actuator tests: everything we drive slopes, skids, a heavy load

Recommendation: A first, with its numbers in vehicle.yaml (a new actuators: section). B only if A isn't close enough to the real car.

The spine in the loop (step 4)

Built 15 Oct. Gazebo can now run the spine's real firmware logic between ego and the car, as the bench does. Off by default; one switch turns it on:

pixi run sim spine:=true          # Gazebo, with the spine in the loop
pixi run sim-check --spine        # proves it: the normal checks, then the spine's stops
 ego ──► output_mux ──/control/gazebo/actuator_cmd──► spine_bridge ──CAN frames──► spine_sim: the spine's own logic
                                                         (arming, 100 ms watchdog, both
                                                          e-stops, its speed loop)
                                                              │ throttle, brake, gear, steering
                                                              ▼
                                gazebo_adapter: the actuator model ──► Gazebo's car
                                                              │ Gazebo's wheels and steering
                                                              ▼
 /vehicle/* ◄── spine_bridge ◄──CAN frames── spine_sim: VEHICLE_FEEDBACK, STATUS, wheel frames
What How
One backend spine_bridge alone reads ego's commands (/control/gazebo/actuator_cmd, from the output mux). gazebo_adapter then only drives the actuator model from the spine's outputs, and no longer publishes /vehicle/*
/system/source still SIM: spine_bridge says so (source:=sim)
The clock spine_sim ticks every 10 ms of Gazebo's time, so the spine's 100 ms watchdog counts Gazebo's time
Arming arm:=true (the default) arms it 12 s after start-up, as the bench does, once the output mux says OPEN (arm_when_open asks each second). After a stop: ros2 service call /spine/arm std_srvs/srv/SetBool "{data: true}". Refused while the safety gate is shut
Hardware e-stop the bench's: /vehicle/bench/hw_estop (true = pressed)
The contract profile spine in docs/interfaces.yaml (pixi run check spine)

Stop times: the direct path vs the spine

Each while driving at 0.8 m/s, Gazebo's road world, Ben's Mac, 15 Oct: three runs of pixi run sim-check --spine, the last one all green. Times are Gazebo's.

Stop Direct path (gazebo_adapter) Spine in the loop
Command stream lost (ego's process stopped) brake 200-220 ms, still 1.24-1.25 s full brake 195-209 ms, still 0.64-0.66 s, and latched until armed again
/safety/estop brake 460 ms (through twist_mux and ego), still 1.70 s brake 1-12 ms (ESTOP_CMD straight to the spine), still 0.18-0.48 s, latched
Hardware e-stop (no such thing) brake 2-10 ms, still 0.46-0.48 s, latched after it's let go
Not armed (always armed) a drive asking 1 m/s for 8 s: no throttle, no motion
  • Why the spine stops sooner: it brakes fully (stop_brake 1.0); the direct path's speed loop only slows to a stop.
  • Its 195-209 ms is spine_bridge's 0.1 s (spine.yaml cmd_timeout) plus the spine's own 0.1 s watchdog: inside the car's 200 ms target (tests.md 3.9).
  • Latched: after any stop, the spine holds the brake, its drive still asking, until someone arms it again. The direct path drives on as soon as commands come back.
  • Found: in two of the three runs, the spine latched once during the first drive: ego's 50 Hz commands stalled for more than 0.1 s (a busy Mac, just after the graph check). That's spine_bridge's cmd_timeout doing its job, but it is tight for a Python ego on a loaded computer. sim-check arms it and drives again, as bench-check does. Worth watching on the car's computer.
