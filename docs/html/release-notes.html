The previous teams' projects: merged in, or replaced

Merged in (each copy has a PROVENANCE.md: the repo, the commit, every change since):

  • AAV_cortex (2025-26): the ESP32 actuator firmware that drives today's car, in firmware/legacy/. Its WiFi password moved out of git. Its UART protocol was rewritten in Python (aav_legacy), and its device logic in Rust (the spine's drivers).
  • Wheel_Encoders (2025-26): the Pico 2 that counts wheel spokes. Its UDP messages are read by encoder_udp.
  • yahboom-IMU (2025-26): the C++ IMU driver, with fixes (yahboom_imu_cpp), and Ben's Rust version of it (aav_imu_yahboom).
  • Nav_Radar (2025-26): the TI radar driver (ti_ros2_driver) and its object clustering (aav_object_detection).
  • Lane_Detection_ROS2 (2025-26): lane following (aav_lane_geometry). The lane finder was never in their repo: we wrote our own.
  • User-Interface (2025-26): the in-car HUD. The Flutter app is in hud/; its Flask back end is aav_hud, moved to our topics.
  • Motor_Steering (2025-26): the steering geometry (Ackermann.m), in vehicle.yaml and docs/reference/.
  • joy_teleop_ackerman (2025-26): its joystick button map, in aav_teleop.
  • 2024-25: the battery monitor, the camera calibrations, and a YOLO stop-sign detector (rewritten as aav_yolo).

Replaced by stock ROS packages:

  • Odometry → robot_localization (two EKFs).
  • The path planners (A, D Lite, the local path generators) → Nav2.
  • Obstacle_Avoidance_logic → Nav2's obstacle layer and collision_monitor.
  • Real-Time-OS (QNX) → twist_mux, the spine's watchdog and ros2 bag.
  • joy_teleop_ackerman → the stock teleop_twist_joy.

Left out:

  • an empty repo, and one its own team abandoned;
  • Motor-Control (a fork of the 2024-25 micro-ROS firmware): a reference only, as the only code that reads the steering's position;
  • a few repos not surveyed yet: Older code.

The car's software, layer by layer

Sensors (aav_sensors starts them, with their settings):

  • IMU: the Yahboom driver, C++ or Rust (imu_driver:=rust), then imu_filter_madgwick for the orientation.
  • GPS: the stock ublox_gps by default (Tentative, Q18); nmea and ublox_dgnss are options.
  • Wheel speed: the 2025-26 encoder Pico, through encoder_udp.
  • Radar: the TI driver. Its settings are still indoor demos.
  • Cameras: usb_cam with the 2024-25 calibrations. Off until the cameras are fitted (Q22).
  • Battery: the 2024-25 battery monitor.

Localization: the EKF filters (robot_localization, settings only):

  • ekf_local: wheel speed and the IMU → odom → base_link, smooth, for steering.
  • ekf_global: the same, plus GPS through navsat_transform → map → odom, for planning.
  • Results: 0.03-0.3 m from the truth on the bench; within 0.2 m parked in Gazebo; a replayed drive matches its recording within 0.03 m.

The drivers (each publishes a command; the mux picks one):

  • Joystick (teleop_twist_joy): a deadman button and an e-stop button. Priority 100. Not yet tried with a real joystick.
  • Keyboard (teleop_twist_keyboard): from any laptop. Priority 90. Works.
  • Scripted (aav_scripted): speed up, hold, slow down. Works.
  • Nav2 (aav_navigation): a planner for cars (SmacPlannerHybrid), Regulated Pure Pursuit, no turning on the spot, GPS waypoints. Reached 8 m and 9.5 m goals in Gazebo, and 40 m past a stop sign on the bench.
  • Lane keeping (aav_lane_finder and aav_lane_geometry): the "AI" slot. On the bench: from 0.3 m off the centre line back to within 5 cm in about 6 s.
  • The stop-sign watcher (aav_yolo): stops the car at a stop sign. Works on the bench.
  • The e-stops: the joystick's button, a laptop's pixi run estop, and the HUD's button. The car's own red button always stops it, whatever the software does.

The mux (twist_mux): one command wins, by priority, and the e-stop locks them all out.

Ego (aav_ego, ours):

  • Turns the winning command into a speed and steering angle the car can really do.
  • The limits every driver gets: 1.0 m/s forward, 0.5 m/s reverse, 1.0 m/s² to speed up, 2.0 m/s² to slow down, a steering rate limit.
  • A stale command stops the car (0.3 s); a computer with the wrong clock gets a warning.

The Ackermann model:

  • Today: ego works out the steering angle from the speed, the turn rate and the wheelbase, for the middle of the front axle. It sends Ackermann commands (AckermannDriveStamped), never twists.
  • Gazebo: its car has Ackermann steering.
  • In progress: both front wheels' real angles, reversing, and the steering curve from the 2025-26 Ackermann.m.

Backends and bridges (exactly one backend runs at a time):

  • spine_bridge: ego's commands → CAN frames, from our CAN contract; the car's status back. Works on the bench, against a pretend spine.
  • legacy_uart_bridge and encoder_udp: today's car: the 2025-26 ESP32 over UART (115200 baud), and the wheel encoder over Ethernet. Not yet tried on the car.
  • The turtle, Gazebo (ros_gz_bridge), CARLA (a stub).
  • The HUD's back end: our topics ↔ the app, over Socket.IO on a WebSocket.
  • foxglove_bridge: Foxglove or Lichtblick on a laptop.

Perception:

  • The detector (aav_yolo): one per computer with a GPU, each under its own name (yolo_detector_orin, yolo_detector_laptop). The stop-sign watcher hears both.
  • The lane finder (aav_lane_finder, new): the camera's picture → the lane lines → /lane_geometry, with OpenCV, in 0.78 ms a picture.
  • The radar clustering (aav_object_detection): works on the pretend radar.
  • Obstacles in Nav2: the radar in the local costmap, and collision_monitor's stop zone. On the bench it stops the car 0.3-0.4 m before a box as wide as the lane. The cameras' layer is off until they're calibrated (Q22).

The HUD:

  • The 2025-26 app, changed for our back end: an e-stop button, Cancel, the camera page, the car's status, the OpenStreetMap credit. On the bench, its e-stop stopped the car in 0.3 s.
  • A bug fixed: the app could never connect to its back end. It now uses WebSocket only.
  • The place database (51 MB): pixi run get-hud-map.
  • Raylib: decided 11 October. The HUD becomes one Rust program, raylib and rclrs, that is also its ROS node. Not started yet.

Firmware

  • The CAN contract (firmware/can/aav.dbc): every frame between ROS and the spine. Python and Rust both test against the same generated vectors.
  • spine-core (Rust): the spine's logic. It boots latched, arms only when asked, stops on a 200 ms gap in the commands, and holds the e-stop. 54 tests.
  • The spine's device drivers (Rust, embedded-hal 1.0):
    • jrk-g2: the steering and brake motor controllers;
    • ad5311: the throttle;
    • pca9555: the I/O expander;
    • ibus: the RC receiver;
    • mcp2515: the CAN chip;
    • spine-board: the glue to spine-core;
    • 137 tests, against pretend hardware.
  • The spine's Pico 2 program (boards/pico2, Embassy): CAN, a 100 ms hardware watchdog, and the e-stop's spare contact (a cut wire reads as pressed). It builds (60 KB) and its UF2 file checks out; never flashed. pixi run spine-flash flashes it.
  • The 2025-26 C++ boards keep running today's car: the ESP32, the wheel encoder Pico, the status display Pico. PlatformIO builds them, in the firmware environment.

Porting to Rust

  • The rule (Ben, 10 Oct): Rust for the firmware and the hardware drivers; our C++ moves to Rust; each language written its own way, not C in Rust.
  • Done:
    • the spine, all in Rust;
    • the ESP32's device logic, as Rust drivers;
    • Ben's Rust IMU driver (rclrs). pixi run imu-compare feeds both drivers the same bytes, and they agree;
    • a first Rust ROS node, as an example (rust-listener).
  • In progress: the Rust IMU driver becomes the default, then the C++ one goes; the radar clustering becomes a Rust node.
  • Next: the board drivers (the wheel encoder and display Picos); later, Python to Rust where it pays off.
  • micro-ROS: none for now. We think again once we see the hardware.

Simulators and bench mode

  • turtlesim (pixi run turtle): a turtle that steers like the car. The first thing to try.
  • Gazebo (pixi run sim): the car in 3D, with Ackermann steering, an IMU and GPS. Nav2 drives it to goals. Its window works on a Mac.
  • Bench mode (pixi run bench): the whole car's software on one computer:
    • a pretend spine;
    • pretend GPS, IMU, wheel speed, camera (a drawn road with lane lines and a stop sign) and radar (boxes);
    • pixi run bench-check: seven checks, plus --perception, --lane and --obstacle.
  • Replay: a recorded drive, run through the EKFs again.
  • CARLA: parked this year: no Jazzy support yet (Q31).
  • Safety: the stand-ins stay on the computer they run on (tools/ros.sh --local), so a pretend car can never reach the real one.

Macs, Pixi, Core ML and MCP

  • Pixi with RoboStack: one pixi.toml gives the same ROS 2 Jazzy on a Mac and on any Linux, with no admin rights.
  • Five environments: the default, ml (PyTorch, YOLO), mcp, firmware (PlatformIO), docs.
  • On a Mac (Apple silicon):
    • it builds in about 15 s, and every test passes;
    • the turtle, Gazebo with its window, Nav2, the Rust example and recording all work;
    • pixi run doctor checks the network before you join the car.
  • Core ML on the Mac:
    • pixi run -e ml export-coreml makes the model (12 s);
    • pixi run -e ml laptop-bench runs the laptop's own detector against the bench;
    • it reads the camera's JPEG pictures, as it will over the car's WiFi.
  • The MCP server (tools/mcp_server.py): lets Claude or another AI app read the docs, the contracts and the live ROS graph. It only reads, and never writes to ROS: a test checks.

Testing

  • pixi run test:
    • it builds everything, then runs Python 230, Rust 208 (plus a build for the Pico 2) and colcon test 291: 0 failures, 5 skipped;
    • also the style check (ament_flake8, ament_pep257) and the HUD app's own tests.
  • The contract check (pixi run check): the running graph against docs/interfaces.yaml (58 topics, 6 services, 5 actions, 9 frames). 0 failures for the turtle, Gazebo, replay and the bench.
  • The bench checks:
    • seven by default: start-up, the contract, the scripted drive, the EKF, both e-stops;
    • then the stop sign, lane keeping and the obstacle.
  • CI (GitHub Actions) is set up to run pixi run test on Linux for every push, and on a Mac every night.
  • Safety checks: the stand-ins can't reach the car; the MCP server can't write.
