### What a CBF filter is

CBF stands for *control barrier function*. Forget the name: it is **a bumper made of
maths**.

```text
   h  =  room to the edge  −  the distance I need to stop  −  a margin

   each tick:  a driver asks for a command
               if that command would make h shrink too fast ─► trim it, as little as possible
               else ─► pass it through unchanged
```

The fence's lock closes when the stopping path comes within 1 m of the edge (plus the pose's
uncertainty). So the filter has to act before that, and `h` includes that margin.

| At 1 m/s: stopping path 1.4 m, margin 1 m | h | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 5 m to the fence | 2.6 m | the command passes |
| 2.6 m to the fence | 0.2 m | the speed is trimmed: the car eases off before the lock |
| 2.3 m to the fence | −0.1 m | the lock (the geofence's NEAR) holds it, as today |

These are simple numbers, on the future spine, with no uncertainty. The full form: [how the
car stops](2026-11-05_how-the-car-stops.md).

| Fact | Why |
|---|---|
| One rule for the fence, a cube, a person and the sign's line | one model of how the car stops |
| A formula first, speed only | no solver on the command path |
| Proposed: it sits before twist_mux, on the automatic drivers' path | ego can't tell who is driving, and a person must never be trimmed |
| It sits beside the locks, never instead | the filter makes the car smooth and early; the locks make it safe |
| It needs measured numbers | otherwise it's a bumper of the wrong size |
